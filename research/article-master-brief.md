# Article Master Brief — RingoStrike

## 1. Working Title

**From Habit Theory to Adaptive Micro-Missions: Designing RingoStrike as an Emotionally Supportive Habit Companion**

Alternative shorter title:

**Designing Adaptive Micro-Missions for Habit Formation: The RingoStrike Case Study**

---

## 2. One-Sentence Summary

This paper presents RingoStrike as an evidence-informed design framework and early product case study for translating habit theory into adaptive micro-missions, emotionally supportive feedback, reminders, and companion-led habit experiences.

---

## 3. Main Thesis

RingoStrike explores how habit-building apps can move beyond static task lists by using small, adaptive, behavior-aware missions supported by an emotionally expressive digital companion. The paper argues that habit-support systems may become more usable and sustainable when they reduce friction, adapt mission intensity to daily user state, and provide feedback that supports autonomy, competence, and emotional continuity. However, the paper does not claim that RingoStrike has already proven long-term habit formation. Its current contribution is a design framework and early case study, not a validated behavioral intervention.

## 5. Paper Type

The correct paper type is:

**Evidence-informed design framework + early case study**

More specifically:

* Not a clinical study
* Not a randomized controlled trial
* Not a proven behavior-change intervention
* Not a pure product description
* Not only a UX portfolio case study

Best current framing:

**A design research paper that proposes a behavior-aware micro-mission framework and demonstrates it through the RingoStrike prototype.**

---

## 6. What This Paper Claims

This paper may claim that:

1. RingoStrike is designed around habit formation concepts, behavior change techniques, adaptive support, gamified feedback, and emotionally supportive companion interaction.

2. The product proposes a structured framework for converting larger goals into smaller missions, tiny missions, bonus missions, and recovery actions.

3. The system uses early behavior signals such as completion, skipping, postponement, tiredness, and inactivity to inform different types of support.

4. Ringo, the companion character, is intended to make reminders, feedback, and progress moments feel more emotionally supportive and less mechanical.

5. The project demonstrates how behavior theory can be translated into concrete UX/product patterns.

6. The framework can be evaluated through a small formative user study focused on usability, perceived support, clarity, motivation, and friction.

7. The paper contributes a practical design model for early-stage habit-building products.

---

## 7. What This Paper Must NOT Claim Yet

This paper must not claim that:

1. RingoStrike has been scientifically proven to build long-term habits.

2. RingoStrike improves mental health, productivity, discipline, or wellbeing in a validated way.

3. The companion character has been proven to increase retention.

4. Micro-missions are proven to work better than normal missions in RingoStrike.

5. Streaks, XP, rewards, or reminders are definitely beneficial for every user.

6. RingoStrike has advanced AI personalization unless that system is actually implemented and evaluated.

7. User testimonials or friend feedback are enough to prove behavioral effectiveness.

8. The app is a therapeutic, medical, psychological, or clinical intervention.

9. The paper provides generalizable conclusions about all habit apps.

10. The design is academically validated before a proper pilot study or user research phase.

Strict wording to use:

**“This paper proposes…”**
**“This early case study explores…”**
**“The framework is designed to…”**
**“Future studies should evaluate…”**

Avoid wording like:

**“This proves…”**
**“Users will…”**
**“RingoStrike changes behavior…”**
**“The app successfully forms habits…”**

## 8. Research Questions

### RQ1

**How can established habit formation and behavior change concepts be translated into micro-mission mechanics for a habit-building app?**

Purpose: connect theory to product design.

---

### RQ2

**How can daily user behavior signals such as completion, skipping, tiredness, inactivity, and postponement inform adaptive mission intensity and supportive feedback?**

Purpose: define the adaptive system.

---

### RQ3

**How do early users perceive the clarity, emotional support, motivation, and friction of RingoStrike’s companion-led micro-mission experience?**

Purpose: prepare for a realistic first user study.

3. A practical early case study showing how a companion character can support habit-building UX.

## 11. RingoStrike Features That Should Appear in the Paper

The paper should discuss these product features:

1. **Paths**

   * Larger life-improvement directions such as focus, health, creativity, or personal growth.

2. **Challenges**

   * Structured programs inside each path.

3. **Daily missions**

   * The normal daily actions users are asked to complete.

4. **Tiny missions**

   * Smaller low-pressure alternatives for tired, overwhelmed, or inactive users.

5. **Bonus missions**

   * Optional extra actions for users with higher energy or stronger momentum.

6. **Mission intensity**

   * Normal, tiny, bonus, recovery, or low-pressure variants.

7. **“Make it smaller” interaction**

   * A key adaptive UX pattern that reduces task difficulty instead of letting the user quit.

8. **“I’m too tired” interaction**

   * A recovery and support signal, not a failure signal.

9. **Streaks**

   * Used carefully as continuity feedback, not as proof of habit formation.

10. **XP and rewards**

* Used as feedback and progress reinforcement.

11. **Step-by-step reward moment**

* Completion confirmation, XP, progress, achievement, streak, and Ringo reaction shown gradually.

12. **Ringo companion**

* Emotionally supportive cat companion with moods such as idle, welcome, thinking, encouraging, warning, proud, sad, sleeping, achievement, and victory.

13. **Adaptive messages**

* Supportive feedback based on mission state and user behavior.

14. **Reminders and notifications**

* Especially Ringo-style reminders that feel caring rather than mechanical.

15. **Mission logs and user behavior signals**

* Completion, skip, remind later, inactive state, streak risk, mission history.

16. **Onboarding and guided flow**

* The app should not force users to search for what to do; Ringo should guide them toward the next small action.

### 4. Behavior-Aware Micro-Mission Framework

This should be the core section.

Proposed model:

1. User behavior signal
2. User state interpretation
3. Mission adaptation
4. Ringo emotional response
5. Feedback/reward moment
6. Next action or reminder

Example:

If user says “I’m too tired” → system interprets low energy → offers tiny mission → Ringo responds supportively → user completes smaller action → reward moment confirms continuity.
